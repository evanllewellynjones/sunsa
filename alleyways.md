## What matters most

1. **Light and shadows** — the most important thing. Emphasize them.
2. **Perspective** — make sure the alleyway makes sense in perspective. The front is generally *wider* and the back *thinner* than you first think.
3. **Detail up front only** — detail belongs in the front; further back, no detail and lost edges.

## More on perspective

- Set the **eye level** and a single vanishing point first; all rooflines, windows and paving lines run to it
- Lightly sketch the main converging lines — buildings first, details later
- Windows and doors get narrower and closer together as they recede

## More on light & shadow

- Alleys are about light contrast: a bright sliver of sky or a sunlit wall against deep shade
- Shadow sides of walls pick up reflected light — warm bounce from sunlit stone, cool from the sky
- Cast shadows across the ground help show the shape of the street

## Character

- Avoid ruler-straight lines everywhere — slightly wobbly edges look more alive
- Add a few details where the eye should go (sign, plant pot, lamp, figure) and leave the rest simple
- Weathered walls: layer a few close colors and scumble or dry-brush over the top
- A small figure gives scale and a focal point
