## [Phase 1: Materials, Mechanics & Surface Prep →](curriculum.html?p=1)

*The chemistry and physical behavior of acrylics, before worrying about composition.*

**1.1. Paint Grade, Viscosity & Rheology**

- Heavy body vs. fluid vs. high-flow acrylics vs. acrylic gouache
- Pigment load, binder behavior, and the wet-to-dry color shift (darkening)
- Single-pigment vs. hue mixtures; reading tube labels (ASTM lightfastness, opacity symbols)

**1.2. Supports & Surface Preparation**

- Stretched canvas vs. linen vs. wood panels (birch/cradled) vs. gesso boards and heavyweight paper
- Surface tooth and absorbency: applying, sanding, and leveling acrylic gesso
- Sealing wood panels (preventing Support Induced Discoloration / SID)

**1.3. Moisture Management & Open Time**

- Water vs. acrylic mediums: under-binding and film integrity
- Managing evaporation with stay-wet palettes, fine-mist atomizers, and slow-drying/retarder mediums
- Brush selection (synthetic stiffness, hog bristle, flats, filberts, riggers, mottlers) and palette knife shapes

## Phase 2: Value, Tonal Structure & Underpainting

*Value does the structural work; color gets the credit. Light and shadow before full chroma.*

**2.1. The Value Scale & Tonal Compression**

- Creating a 9-step grayscale value chart in acrylic
- How acrylic's compressed dynamic range compares to natural light
- Squinting; simplifying complex scenes into 3–4 core value masses (Notan and value thumbnails)

**2.2. Toned Grounds & Imprimatura**

- Why paint on a toned surface (killing the stark white, establishing mid-tones immediately)
- Choosing ground temperature: warm earth tones (Burnt Sienna, Raw Umber, Yellow Ochre) vs. cool/neutral grounds
- Wipe-out techniques (subtractive value mapping while the wash is still open)

**2.3. Monochromatic Underpainting (Grisaille & Verdaccio)**

- Blocking in the structural darks and shadow shapes first
- Form through light logic: highlight, light mass, halftone, core shadow, reflected light, cast shadow
- Judging value accuracy before moving to local color

## Phase 3: Color Theory, Mixing & Temperature

*From theoretical color wheels to practical palette control and atmospheric harmony.*

**3.1. Hue, Value, and Chroma (Munsell Concepts in Practice)**

- Separating a color's *value* (lightness/darkness) from its *chroma* (saturation/intensity)
- Lightening or darkening a color without killing its saturation or shifting its temperature unintentionally (Titanium White vs. Zinc White vs. chromatic lightening)

**3.2. Palette Strategies & Split Primaries**

- The warm/cool split-primary palette (why a single red, yellow, and blue creates muddy secondaries)
- The Zorn palette and limited earth palettes for tonal cohesion
- Integrating convenience greens and earth tones (Yellow Ochre, Burnt Umber, Raw Sienna)

**3.3. Chromatic Darks & Neutralizing (Avoiding Mud)**

- Abandoning tube black: mixing rich darks (e.g., Ultramarine Blue + Burnt Umber, Phthalo Blue + Alizarin/Quinacridone Crimson, Dioxazine Purple + Sap Green)
- Using complements to intentionally desaturate and create chromatic grays
- Controlling high-chroma pigments (Phthalos, Quinacridones) so they don't overpower a mix

**3.4. Color Temperature & Harmonious Schemes**

- Relative temperature: making a cool color feel warm in context (and vice versa)
- Warm light / cool shadows vs. cool light / warm shadows
- Harmony frameworks: analogous, complementary, split-complementary, and triadic dominance

## Phase 4: Brushwork, Edges & Acrylic-Specific Techniques

*Working with acrylic's fast drying time to get varied textures, soft transitions, and optical depth.*

**4.1. Edge Control (The Secret to Realism & Depth)**

- The four edge types: hard, firm, soft, and lost
- Wet-into-wet blending vs. damp-brush feathering before the film skins over
- Hard edges to pull focal points forward; soft/lost edges to push elements back

**4.2. Indirect Painting: Glazing & Scumbling**

- **Glazing:** transparent pigments in gloss/satin glazing liquid to shift temperature, deepen shadows, or unify a passage without disturbing underlying brushwork
- **Scumbling:** scrubbing semi-opaque or opaque lighter mixes (dry-brush or medium-thinned) over darker or textured underlayers for haze, mist, or distance
- Preventing clouding in dark glazes: gloss vs. matte medium binders

**4.3. Direct Painting: Alla Prima, Impasto & Knife Work**

- Brush economy: lay down the right mark and leave it alone (no overworking or lifting drying paint)
- Directional brushwork: following the cross-contour and plane changes of the form
- Heavy-body and gel medium impasto: palette knife application, broken color, tactile surface relief

## Phase 5: Composition, Perspective & Spatial Depth

*Designing paintings that guide the eye and create a convincing three-dimensional illusion on a flat plane.*

**5.1. Compositional Armatures & Focal Hierarchy**

- Rule of thirds, dynamic symmetry, golden ratio, and classic armatures (S-curve, L-design, steelyard balance, radiating lines)
- A clear focal point through maximum contrast, sharpest edges, and highest chroma
- Lead-in lines, eye-path manipulation, and avoiding visual tangents

**5.2. Linear Perspective & Structural Drawing**

- Horizon line (eye level), vanishing points (1-point, 2-point, inclined planes)
- Sight-size, comparative measurement, and plumb lines for accurate proportions
- Integrating architecture and man-made structures naturally into organic environments

**5.3. Aerial (Atmospheric) Perspective**

- How atmosphere alters value, edge sharpness, and color temperature over distance
- Receding planes: neutralizing greens and shifting distant mountains without turning skies or backgrounds chalky
- Overlap and scale shift to separate foreground, middle ground, and background

## Phase 6: Subject-Specific Application Modules

*Applying the foundations to classic painting subjects.*

**6.1. Skies, Clouds & Water**

- Smooth, gradated skies (zenith-to-horizon shifts without greening or streaking)
- Cloud volume, silver linings, shadowed undersides, and varied soft/wispy edges
- Still-water reflections (vertical alignment, value darkening) vs. moving water, waves, and translucent shallows

**6.2. Landscape Elements: Rocks, Mountains & Trees**

- **Rocks & cliffs:** faceting planes, palette knife for fracture lines, warm/cool plane transitions
- **Trees & foliage:** grouping leaf masses by value first (avoiding "lollipop" trees), sky-holes, bark texture, varied greens

**6.3. Still Life, Florals & Material Textures**

- Transparency (glass), specular highlights (metal/ceramics), and soft drapery folds
- Floral structure: flower heads as spheres/cones in light before detailing individual petals

**6.4. Figure, Portrait & Anatomical Mass**

- Structural block-in of the head and figure (planes of the head, écorché/muscular massing)
- Flesh tones across different lighting environments (warm/cool zones of the face)

## Phase 7: Critique, Revision & Archival Finishing

*Knowing when a painting is finished, fixing mistakes, and protecting the work.*

**7.1. Self-Critique & Problem Solving**

- Diagnosing a stalled painting: value compression, muddy transitions, competing focal points
- Acrylic rescue: scraping back, sanding cured passages, isolating areas to repaint

**7.2. Isolation Coats & Varnishing**

- Why apply a permanent, non-removable isolation coat (protects paint during future varnish removal, unifies surface sheen)
- Choosing and applying archival varnish (MSA/solvent-based vs. waterborne polymer; gloss, satin, matte combinations)
