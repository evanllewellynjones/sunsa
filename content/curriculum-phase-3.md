## 3.1. Hue, Value, and Chroma (Munsell Concepts in Practice)

*Three separate dials on every color.*

**Core concepts**

- **Hue** is the color family; **value** is light/dark; **chroma** is intensity. Most mixing errors come from changing one by accident while adjusting another.
- **Lightening without killing chroma:** Titanium White is opaque and strong — it lightens fast but chalks colors and cools them. Zinc or a transparent mixing white is gentler. Lightening with a lighter color of similar hue (e.g., Cadmium Yellow into Cadmium Orange) keeps chroma higher.
- **Darkening without muddying:** reach for a darker neighbor hue or the complement in small amounts rather than black.

## 3.2. Palette Strategies & Split Primaries

*Choosing tubes on purpose.*

**Core concepts**

- **Split-primary palette:** a warm and a cool version of each primary — e.g., Cadmium Yellow Light / Cadmium Yellow Medium, Cadmium Red / Quinacridone Magenta, Ultramarine / Phthalo Blue. Mix secondaries from the two primaries that lean toward each other for clean results; a single red, yellow, and blue leaves some secondaries dull.
- **Zorn palette:** Yellow Ochre, Cadmium Red, black, white. The black acts as a weak blue, giving a tight, harmonious range — great training for value and temperature.
- **Convenience colors:** Yellow Ochre, Raw Sienna, Burnt Umber and a ready-made green save time, especially for landscape. Know what pigments they contain.

## 3.3. Chromatic Darks & Neutralizing (Avoiding Mud)

*Rich darks, living grays, and taming strong pigments.*

**Core concepts**

- **Chromatic darks:** Ultramarine + Burnt Umber, Phthalo Blue + Quinacridone Crimson, or Dioxazine Purple + Sap Green make darks you can push warm or cool — tube black flattens them.
- **Chromatic grays:** mix complements (blue/orange, red/green, yellow/violet) plus white. Tip the balance toward one side to keep the gray warm or cool.
- **High-tinting pigments:** Phthalos and Quinacridones overpower mixes. Add them to the other color a tiny amount at a time, never the reverse.
- **Mud** is usually too many pigments, or three primaries in uneven amounts. Stick to two or three pigments per mix.

**Video lessons**

## 3.4. Color Temperature & Harmonious Schemes

*Warm and cool are relative — and they create light.*

**Core concepts**

- **Relative temperature:** a color is only warm or cool next to another. Alizarin is a cool red next to Cadmium Red but warm next to Ultramarine.
- **Light and shadow temperature:** under warm light (sun, lamps), shadows read cool; under cool light (overcast, north window), shadows read warm. It's a strong tendency, not a law — always check the subject.
- **Harmony frameworks:** analogous (neighbors), complementary, split-complementary, and triadic. Let one temperature or hue family dominate and use its opposite as an accent.

## Phase 3 Studio Exercises

### Exercise 1 · Tint & Chroma Ladders

**Objective:** see how different whites and methods change a color.

1. Pick one strong color (Cadmium Red or Phthalo Green).
2. Row A: lighten it in 5 steps with Titanium White.
3. Row B: lighten it in 5 steps with Zinc or transparent mixing white.
4. Row C: lighten it with a lighter, related hue (e.g., Cadmium Orange → Cadmium Yellow) before any white.
5. Row D: lower its chroma in 5 steps by adding its complement, keeping value constant.

**What to observe:** which rows stay saturated and which go chalky or shift temperature.

### Exercise 2 · Split-Primary Secondary Chart

**Objective:** prove why the split-primary palette mixes cleaner.

1. Lay out warm and cool versions of each primary.
2. Mix every yellow with every blue, every red with every yellow, every red with every blue — a grid of greens, oranges, and violets.
3. Label each swatch with the two pigments used.

**What to observe:** the cleanest secondaries come from primaries that lean toward each other; the dullest from primaries that lean away.

### Exercise 3 · Chromatic Darks & Gray Strips

**Objective:** replace tube black for good.

1. Mix three chromatic darks: Ultramarine + Burnt Umber, Phthalo Blue + Quinacridone Crimson, Dioxazine Purple + Sap Green.
2. For each, paint a strip from the dark out to white in 5 steps.
3. Next to each, paint a strip of Mars Black + white for comparison.
4. Then mix a complementary gray (blue + orange + white) and push it warmer and cooler in 3 steps each way.

**What to observe:** the chromatic strips keep color identity in the tints; the black strip goes dead gray.

### Exercise 4 · Same Scene, Two Temperatures

**Objective:** feel how temperature sets mood and light.

1. Choose a simple subject — a box or a house shape on a table.
2. Paint it twice at 5"×7": once with warm light and cool shadows, once with cool light and warm shadows.
3. Keep the values identical between the two; change only temperature.

**What to observe:** how completely the time of day and mood change with values held constant.
