*Acrylic dries fast — this phase turns that into an advantage. Control edges, build depth with thin layers, and make confident direct marks. Finish the studio exercises at the bottom before moving to Phase 5.*

## 4.1. Edge Control (The Secret to Realism & Depth)

*Where two shapes meet decides where the eye goes.*

**Core concepts**

- **Four edge types:** hard (crisp break), firm (slightly softened), soft (gradual blend), and lost (two shapes merge into one).
- **Acrylic blending:** wet-into-wet works only while both passages are open — use a wet palette, retarder, or a light mist. Once the film starts to skin over, switch to damp-brush feathering or a thin scumble across the edge.
- **Depth through edges:** hard edges pull forward and attract attention; soft and lost edges recede. Save the hardest edges for the focal point.

## 4.2. Indirect Painting: Glazing & Scumbling

*Layers that change color optically.*

**Core concepts**

- **Glazing:** a transparent pigment thinned with gloss or satin glazing medium, laid over dry paint. Use it to shift temperature, deepen shadows, or unify a passage without disturbing the brushwork below.
- **Scumbling:** the opposite — a thin, semi-opaque, lighter layer scrubbed or dry-brushed over a darker or textured underlayer. Creates haze, mist, atmosphere, and distance.
- **Avoiding cloudy darks:** matte medium contains matting agents that can make dark glazes look milky. Use gloss medium for dark glazes; adjust sheen at the varnish stage.

## 4.3. Direct Painting: Alla Prima, Impasto & Knife Work

*One confident mark beats five timid ones.*

**Core concepts**

- **Brush economy:** load the brush fully, place the stroke, and leave it. Going back into half-dry acrylic lifts and tears the film.
- **Directional brushwork:** stroke along or across the form's planes — across a cylinder, down a falling plane — so the marks themselves describe shape.
- **Impasto and knife work:** heavy-body paint, or paint extended with gel medium, holds knife and brush texture. Use broken color (unmixed strokes side by side) and tactile relief to catch light at the focal point.

## Phase 4 Studio Exercises

### Exercise 1 · Four-Edge Sampler

**Objective:** produce every edge type on purpose, in acrylic.

1. Paint four 3"×3" squares, each a dark shape meeting a light background.
2. Square 1: hard edge. Square 2: firm (one light pass of a damp brush). Square 3: soft (wet-into-wet blend while both are open). Square 4: lost (match values so the shape dissolves into the background).
3. Note which method worked for each, and how long you had.

**What to observe:** the lost edge is almost entirely a value decision, not a blending one.

### Exercise 2 · Glaze & Scumble Overlay

**Objective:** change color without repainting.

1. Paint a simple, opaque value study of a landscape (sky, hills, foreground) and let it dry.
2. Glaze the foreground with a transparent warm (Quinacridone Gold or Burnt Sienna in gloss medium).
3. Glaze the shadows with transparent Ultramarine.
4. Scumble a light blue-gray thinly over the distant hills.
5. Compare against a photo of the study taken before step 2.

**What to observe:** glazes deepen and warm; scumbles push things back and add air.

### Exercise 3 · Ten-Stroke Still Life

**Objective:** train brush economy.

1. Set up one simple object — an apple, a cup, an egg.
2. Paint it on a 5"×7" panel in no more than 10 brushstrokes per object (background excluded).
3. Repeat three times, each time with fewer, better-placed strokes.

**What to observe:** the later attempts look fresher and more confident even with less information.

### Exercise 4 · Knife-Only Impasto Study

**Objective:** build texture and broken color without a brush.

1. Mix heavy-body paint with an equal amount of gel medium.
2. Paint a simple subject (a rock, a flower, a tree) using only a palette knife.
3. Keep the thickest paint in the lights at the focal point; keep shadows thin.
4. Let it cure fully (impasto can take several days) and view it under raking light.

**What to observe:** thick paint in the lights and thin paint in the shadows makes the lights advance.
