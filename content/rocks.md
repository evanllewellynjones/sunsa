## Building the form

- Think of rocks as **blocks with planes** — top (lightest), side facing the light, shadow side
- Block in the dark shadow shape first, then mid-tone, then lights on top
- Use a palette knife or the edge of a flat brush for crisp, angular edges

## Color

- Rocks reflect their surroundings: blue-violet from the sky in shadow, green near foliage, warm near sand
- Mix greys from complements (Burnt Sienna + Ultramarine) rather than black + white
- Add small cracks and crevices only in the light areas and near the focal point

## Grounding

- Give every rock a dark contact shadow where it meets the ground or water so it doesn't float
- Vary sizes — one large, a few medium, many small — and overlap them
- Rocks in water: reflection is darker and slightly broken by ripples

## Shadow mixes

*Also on the [Shadows](topic.html?t=shadows) study.*

Stones are rarely uniform gray; their shadows pick up ambient sky light and ground reflections:

1. **Warm cast shadows** — Raw Umber + Ultramarine Blue + Unbleached (or Buff) Titanium. Soft, neutral, chalky grays that keep the gritty texture of limestone, mortar or sandstone without turning stark.
2. **Cool ambient wall shadows** — Cobalt (or Ultramarine) Blue + Burnt Sienna + Titanium White. Burnt Sienna warms the blue into slate grays; leaning bluer simulates skylight reflecting onto vertical walls.

## Granite vs. slate

Granite is coarse and made of several minerals (feldspar, quartz, mica), so its highlights are broken, speckled and warm-to-neutral. Slate is fine-grained and splits in sheets, so its highlights are sharp, flat-planed and cool.

| | Granite | Slate |
|---|---|---|
| Edges | Broken, irregular | Crisp, razor-thin, geometric |
| Planes | Rounded, fractured crests | Stepped ledges, parallel sheets |
| Color | Warm earth mottled with cool greys | Cool slate blues, thin rust bands |
| Transitions | Gradual, diffuse | Hard value jumps at fractures |
| Brushwork | Dry-brush, scumble, stipple with a stiff flat; drag across the tooth so paint hits only high points | Chisel edge of a flat, a rigger, or the edge of a knife; straight, deliberate strokes |

## Building the rock (acrylic)

### 1. Underpainting

- Thin, lean wash (water or matte medium) that dries flat and matte
- Block in the whole silhouette, darkest at the base and in deep fissures

| Stone | Mix | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Granite | Raw Umber + Ultramarine + touch of Burnt Sienna | Warm chromatic black; avoids the dead look of Ivory Black |
| Slate | Payne's Grey + Raw (or Burnt) Umber, **3:1** | Inky slate-black that separates the planes |

### 2. Granite mid-tones

Granite has no flat planes; build coarse, mottled mass.

| Step | Mix | How |
|---|---|---|
| Local tone | Burnt Umber + Unbleached Titanium + touch of Ultramarine | Planes between shadow and light; choppy marks, don't blend |
| Weathered crust | Raw Sienna + Raw Umber + Unbleached Titanium | Scumble over sunlit facets with the flat of a stiff bristle brush; let the underpainting show in the recesses |
| Mottle | Two piles: pale grey (Ultramarine + Burnt Sienna + White) and warm buff (Raw Sienna + Buff) | Dab or stipple with a splayed brush or sponge across lit planes to suggest mineral grain |

### 3. Slate mid-tones

Slate is geometric. First sort every facet into shadow, skylight plane or lit plane.

| Step | Mix | How |
|---|---|---|
| Shadow planes | Payne's Grey + Neutral Grey + speck of Burnt Umber | Flat strokes parallel to the layering; chisel edge keeps boundaries sharp |
| Weathered face | Cobalt Blue + Raw Umber + Unbleached Titanium | Broad, flat swaths; gives a greenish quarry-stone grey |
| Rust bands | Burnt Sienna + Raw Umber, thinned with glaze medium | Thin rigger lines along the cleavage to break up the blue-grey |

## Highlights

Pure white or bright yellow flattens a rock and turns chalky. Match the highlight to the stone and to the light: warm sun, cool sky, or surroundings.

### By light and setting

| Light | Mix | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Warm sun | Raw Sienna + Unbleached Titanium | Sun-bleached stone; Unbleached Titanium is less chalky than Titanium White |
| Warm sun | Burnt Sienna + Naples Yellow | Warm granite, sedimentary layers |
| Warm sun | Burnt Umber + speck of Cadmium Orange + White | Lifts mid-tone browns into sunlit ridges |
| Warm sun | Yellow Ochre + pale pink (Venetian Red or Quinacridone Burnt Orange + White) | Dusty rim light on sandstone, red clay |
| Cool sky | Cobalt (or Ultramarine) + Unbleached Titanium | Grey-blue skylight on upward-facing ridges |
| Cool sky | Cerulean + Raw Umber + White | Slate-teal: shale, wet river boulders, limestone |
| Cool sky | Payne's Grey + Titanium White | Crisp glints along fractures on slate or basalt |
| Moss / lichen | Sap Green + Naples Yellow + White | Dry lichen on crests |
| Moss / lichen | Olive Green + Raw Sienna | Semi-shaded, damp boulders |
| Moss / lichen | Nickel Azo Yellow + touch of Phthalo Green + Buff | Fresh moss on sunlit facets |
| Distance | Quinacridone Violet (or Dioxazine) + Yellow Ochre + White | Mauve-greys for distant mountain rock in late light |
| Ground bounce | Raw Sienna or Peach, dry-brushed on undersides | Rock sitting on sand or dry grass |

### Granite and slate recipes

| Target | Mix | Ratio | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Granite, sunlit facets | Buff + Burnt Sienna + Ultramarine | 80:15:5 | Ultramarine keeps the Sienna from going terracotta. Break into 2–3 micro-tints, not one flat tint |
| Granite, quartz sparkle | Titanium White + pinprick of Raw Umber (or Yellow Ochre) | — | Fine stipples or broken lines along crests |
| Granite, lichen crests | Naples Yellow + Raw Umber + Sap Green | 70:25:5 | Pale, dusty crust lichen |
| Slate, dry chipped ridges | White + Payne's Grey + Burnt Umber | 70:20:10 | Umber neutralizes the blue into flint grey; use on razor edges |

### Applying highlights

- **Dry-brush:** stiff flat or bright, heavy-body paint, wipe the excess on a towel, skim so paint catches only raised grain and edges
- **Edge hardness:** sharp, high-contrast highlights on fractures; soft highlights on rounded, river-worn stone
