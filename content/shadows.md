> **Water the color down significantly** and paint over an entire area — the area falls into shadow (or shifts in temperature) but the shapes underneath still show.

## Sky

### Washes to calm the sky

1. **Toning down warm — Raw Sienna or Yellow Ochre.** A whisper of warm earth tone cancels the stark, chalky glare of white-blue sky paint without turning it green (keep it very transparent). It lends a natural, unified sunlight tone that instantly softens high contrast.
   - *Caution:* keep the ratio extremely sheer. If the wash is too heavy with yellow pigment, it reacts with the blue underneath and reads greenish.
2. **Overcast — Burnt Umber or Raw Umber + a speck of Ultramarine Blue.** Raw Umber is a natural desaturator. A faint veil of umber neutralizes vibrant blue and knocks down white values into a calm, soft daylight gray.
3. **Dusty — Burnt Sienna + a tiny pinprick of Quinacridone Magenta or Alizarin Crimson.** Terrific for late-afternoon landscapes. Creates a subtle peachy/rosy warmth near the horizon that visually calms intense sky glare.

### Zenith & horizon

Skies in nature are rarely a uniform sheet:

- **The zenith (top)** — if the top is too bright, apply a slightly deeper, cooler transparent blue glaze (**Ultramarine Blue** or **Cobalt Blue**) with no white. Darkening the upper edge creates a natural atmospheric vignette that keeps the eye from escaping off the top of the board.
- **The horizon (bottom)** — if the sky near the tree line is competing with the trees, apply a sheer **Raw Sienna** or warm neutral wash, gradated upward from the horizon line, to soften the stark silhouette contrast against pushed-back trees.

### Applying a sky wash

1. **Use medium, not water** — a wide sky area shows water streaking immediately. Use an acrylic glazing liquid or satin medium to keep an even, streak-free film across broad surfaces.
2. **Soft, wide synthetic flat brush** — load lightly, lay down broad horizontal strokes across the sky, and gently feather out with a dry mop brush or soft shop cloth before it tacks up.
3. **Work wet-on-dry** — make sure the background trees and first sky layer are bone dry so you don't lift or blend paint into the glaze.

## Trees

### Washes to push trees back

Distant air scatters shorter blue wavelengths, so a cool glaze over vibrant greens cuts their warmth and moves them into the middle-to-far distance:

1. **Standard daylight — Ultramarine Blue or Cobalt Blue + a tiny touch of Titanium White (or Zinc White) + a hint of Burnt Sienna** to knock back raw vividness.
2. **Warm afternoon / golden hour — Cobalt Blue or French Ultramarine + a speck of Raw Umber or Yellow Ochre + white.** Keeps the trees receding without turning warm evening light into an unnatural icy tone.

### Shadows on greenery & foliage

Adding black to green yields muddy, chalky olive tones. Instead, shift the temperature and lower the value:

1. **Sap Green (or Phthalo Green) + Burnt Sienna** — a warm, earthy woodland shadow that feels organic and sun-dappled.
2. **Cadmium Yellow/Green mix + Dioxazine Purple** — purple is the direct complement of yellow; a touch cleanly cuts the saturation of bright leaf greens for soft ambient leaf shadows.
3. **Ultramarine Blue + Yellow Ochre + touch of Burnt Umber** — a classic muted green-gray for midground tree masses and the undersides of foliage clusters.

### Applying a tree wash

1. **Avoid water-only thinning** — too much water breaks the acrylic binder, giving a blotchy, underbound wash that can lift or dry unevenly. Use **glazing medium** (matte, satin or gloss to match your finish) to keep pigment evenly suspended.
2. **Soft-edge the contours** — when washing over the tree line, gently feather the hard silhouette edges against the sky with a clean, damp synthetic brush. Softer edges recede faster than crisp outlines.
3. **Layer gradually** — acrylics dry slightly darker than they look wet. Start with a much thinner wash than you think you need; a second glaze can go on once the first is dry in a couple of minutes.

## Rocks, stonework & masonry

Stones are rarely uniform gray; their shadows pick up ambient sky light and ground reflections:

1. **Warm cast shadows** — Raw Umber + Ultramarine Blue + Unbleached (or Buff) Titanium. Soft, neutral, chalky grays that keep the gritty texture of limestone, mortar or sandstone without turning stark.
2. **Cool ambient wall shadows** — Cobalt (or Ultramarine) Blue + Burnt Sienna + Titanium White. Burnt Sienna warms the blue into slate grays; leaning bluer simulates skylight reflecting onto vertical walls.

## White & light surfaces

Timbers, pillars, trim. White in shadow is never white mixed with black — it carries the temperature of its surroundings:

1. **Daylight skylight shadows** — Ultramarine Blue + Titanium White + touch of Raw Umber. Surfaces turned away from the sun receive cool light from the blue sky dome; the hint of umber keeps it from looking like blue paint.
2. **Reflected ground light** — Burnt Sienna + Cobalt Blue + Titanium White. Near warm stone or dry earth, light bounces up into the shadow side, giving the gray a subtle warm peach or buff undertone.

## Deepest shadows (any subject)

1. **Burnt Umber + Ultramarine Blue** — the ultimate landscape workhorse. Mixed roughly 50/50 it yields a deep, velvety neutral dark that can be pushed warm (more umber) or cool (more blue). Naturally transparent; mimics atmospheric darkness.
2. **Dioxazine Purple + Raw Umber** — an exceptionally rich, bottomless dark with a subtle violet resonance. Excellent for dense foliage cores and shaded timber joints.
3. **Phthalo Green + Alizarin Crimson** — complementary opposites that neutralize into an intense, glassy near-black. Lean magenta for warm shadows, green for cool, reflective water shadows.

## Rules of thumb

- **Opposite temperature rule** — if the direct light is warm (golden sunlight), shadows generally read cool (bluish, violet or neutral gray) from sky reflection and eye perception.
- **Value first, color second** — shadows must hang together in value. A shadow too light, or speckled with bright spots, destroys the illusion of a solid plane.
- **Keep shadows thinner** — apply highlights thick and opaque; keep deep shadows fluid or semi-transparent with medium. Transparency lets light into the paint film, giving depth instead of a solid barrier.
