### [Hard & soft edges in portraits](https://youtu.be/ma-knl2Bu6s)

- Edges can't all be the same: **hard, soft, lost**
- Lost edges are the background
- Hard edges attract the eye — there should not be that many

### [5 tips to hard & soft edges](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=giUACK8XwDI&t=228s)

- An edge is the line between colors or values
- Hard & soft edges create depth
- Use hard & soft edges to direct the viewer's eye
- Cameras always give you *all* hard edges, so don't copy the camera
- Light & dark values meeting almost always create a hard edge
- Edges inside of a shadow are almost always soft
- Be deliberate with edges

### Technique in edging

**[5 methods of blending](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C0YkHg52k-4)**

1. **Basic feathering** — two colors next to each other, feather with a dry brush
2. **Wet on dry** — let a color dry, then paint over it and feather the wet onto the dry
3. **Dry brush / scumbling** — very light paint brushed lightly over the top; blends, but *does leave streaks, not totally combined*
4. **Wet on wet** — different shades smudged together while wet. **Use retarder** to help
5. **Glazing** — transparent layer over a dry layer; use a soft brush
   - Use water — or better, glazing medium — over a dry paint area. Transparent paints are easiest to glaze with
   - You can wipe glazing off parts of the painting (e.g. less in the foreground)
   - Multiple glazes in different areas are fine — but let each **dry**
   - [Glazing — how to](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8g-_7OWQTQw)

**Other edge tools**

- Tape creates a very hard edge
- Dabbing creates a soft edge
- Hard-edge outlining in black creates a cartoonish look — stay away from very dark outlining

**Creating blends / soft edges**

- Use medium and water, then dry brush together
- Lay down several colors that fall between the darkest and lightest — multiple layers, not just two colors rubbed together ([using layers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kNN2dHWKKaQ))
- A watery, semi-transparent color over the top also softens — though the hard edge may show through
- Soft edge wash
- Hard edge to soft edge
