*Glazing = a thin, transparent layer of color over paint that is already **dry**. It changes the color, value or temperature underneath without covering it. (For how sharp or soft a meeting between two shapes is, see [Edges](topic.html?t=edges).)*

### What a glaze does

- **Deepens shadows** without going muddy or flat
- **Shifts temperature** — a cool blue glaze pushes things back; a warm gold glaze brings them forward
- **Unifies** a busy area or the whole painting under one light
- **Adds glow** — light passes through the glaze and bounces back off the layer below
- **Softens** an edge or detail from on top, without repainting it

### Notes — [Glazing — how to](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8g-_7OWQTQw)

- Transparent layer over a **dry** layer; use a **soft brush**
- Use water — or better, **glazing medium** — over a dry paint area. Transparent paints are easiest to glaze with
- You can **wipe glazing off** parts of the painting (e.g. less in the foreground)
- Multiple glazes in different areas are fine — but let each **dry**

### Mixing the glaze

- **Medium, not water** — too much water breaks the acrylic binder and gives a blotchy, streaky film that can lift. Glazing medium keeps pigment evenly suspended
- **Start very thin** — roughly 1 part paint to 5–10 parts medium; you can always add a second glaze
- **Match the sheen** — gloss medium keeps color rich and saturated; matte can turn cloudy if built up in many layers
- **Acrylics dry darker** than they look wet — judge a glaze after it dries

### Transparent vs opaque pigments

| Glaze well (transparent) | Avoid for glazing (opaque) |
|---|---|
| Phthalo Blue / Phthalo Green | Titanium White |
| Quinacridone Magenta, Red, Gold | Cadmium Reds, Oranges, Yellows |
| Ultramarine Blue (semi) | Naples Yellow |
| Dioxazine Purple | Cerulean Blue (semi-opaque) |
| Transparent Red / Yellow Oxide, Burnt Sienna | Any color mixed with white |

**Quick test:** paint a black marker stripe on scrap, brush the color over it. If the stripe still shows clearly, it's transparent.

### Step by step

1. **Let the layer underneath dry completely** — touch-dry isn't enough on thick areas
2. **Mix** a small amount of transparent color into medium
3. **Brush on** with a soft, wide synthetic brush in one even pass — don't scrub
4. **Lift or wipe** with a damp rag or dry brush where you want less (highlights, foreground)
5. **Dry, then judge** — repeat with another thin glaze if needed

### Common mistakes

- Glazing over **wet or tacky** paint — lifts and smears the layer below
- **Too much pigment** — it stops being a glaze and becomes a flat coat of paint
- **Going back over** a glaze as it starts to set — leaves streaks and brush marks
- Glazing with **white mixed in** — goes chalky and kills the glow
